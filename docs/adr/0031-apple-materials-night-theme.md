# ADR 0031 — The night theme follows Apple's dark materials

**Status:** accepted (2026-10-18); its night accent is superseded by ADR 0036, and its drawn indigo wallpaper by ADR 0037; below 1024 px it is extended by ADR 0050 (the phone layout). Supersedes the night palette and the system font of ADR 0030; the rest of ADR 0030 (paper theme, display pair, named type scale, structure felt not seen, states as ink, the inset sheet, the framed shell) stands.

## Context

ADR 0030 gave the night theme opaque slate surfaces with a sage marker. The maintainer then pointed to the Materials page of Apple's macOS 27 UI kit: a deep indigo wallpaper with violet light, and frosted panels in five thicknesses that take their colour from what is behind them, with text set as white at four strengths. A preview of that treatment injected into the live app, in Arabic, was the version chosen.

## Decision

- **A wallpaper is the window's background.** In the night theme `html` carries a fixed deep indigo gradient with violet light and sweeping arcs of light like the macOS wallpaper, so the frost has something to blur (`--wallpaper`, `--wallpaper-base`); the menu sits directly on it.
- **Glass is the navigation layer, never the content** (Apple, *Adopting Liquid Glass*: glass "applies to the topmost layer of the interface, where you define your navigation", sidebars float in it, and glass is not layered on glass). From 1024 px the menu and the page are two panels inset from the window with concentric corners. The menu is the one large glass plane: a neutral grey frost (`--surface` at 70 %), lighter than the wallpaper the way Apple's materials are, blurring what is behind it (`blur(72px) saturate(120%)`; saturation is kept low so the indigo glow does not tint the grey) so the wallpaper's light becomes soft clouds, with a faint grain (`--frost-grain`) that makes it read as frosted rather than tinted. The page card was first Apple's ultra-thick content material; the maintainer chose to give it the same frost as the menu instead (`--canvas` equal to `--surface`, the same blur and grain), so the two panels read as one material. This departs from Apple's advice to keep content off glass, accepted because every text token is measured at the wallpaper's brightest point and still clears 4.5:1. Menus, selects and dialogs are controls and take a light glass (`blur(28px)`). Only the wallpaper is indigo; every panel is neutral grey, as in Apple's kit; the list bar is the page's scroll edge effect. The record panel is a sheet that holds a form and is solid (`--elevated`), so rows never ghost through its fields.
- **The two navigations are one family.** A list's views rail is the main menu's sibling: a rounded inset panel inside the page card with the same corner radius, and the same pill rows, icon weight, current-item ring and hover wash. It is solid, not glass, because it sits on the page.
- **`backdrop-filter` is written once, unprefixed.** The CSS pipeline adds Safari's prefix itself; writing both made it keep only the prefixed one, which Chrome ignores, so the glass silently never rendered in Chrome.
- **Text is white at strengths, not greys.** Primary 96 %, secondary 80 %, tertiary 72 %; edges are white at 14 %, the raised step white at 8 %, and the glass catches light along its top edge.
- **The primary action is Apple's own button, as apple.com draws it:** a capsule in Apple blue (`#0071e3`) with regular-weight white text (4.7:1) and no shadow, one per view. Hover and press darken a step (`#006edb`, `#0064c8`) because Apple's lighter hover blue falls below 4.5:1; press also scales to 98 %. An indigo fill was tried first and read as generic.
- **The search palette is Spotlight in Liquid Glass.** It opens from the header launcher or ⌘K / Ctrl K as a 680 px panel with 22 px corners in the upper fifth of the screen. Opening it blurs and lightly dims the page behind; the panel is clear glass over that field (a thin tint, a saturated blur, a sheen across its top half, a bright specular rim and a deep shadow). Its field is 20 px Medium with no browser clear button; result groups are ruled, semibold sentence-case heads; a result's secondary line sits under its title on the same start edge, its direction isolated so a date never jumps to the far side in Arabic; the selected result is a soft lighter fill inside a fine rim; matching words are set semibold, whole words only, so a weight change never splits an Arabic word's joining. On a phone it stays a bottom sheet.
- **Apple's system hues carry meaning.** The marks keep Apple's values (green `#30d158`, orange `#ff9f0a`, red `#ff453a`); the words take a lighter step of the same hue (`#7aeba2`, `#ffc670`, `#ffa8a1`), and the accent is a light indigo (`#c4c2ff`) over an indigo tint. Contrast is measured at the unblurred peak of a light band, the worst a reader can meet, where every text token clears 4.5:1 on the canvas, the raised step and an inset sheet (tertiary text 4.6:1, the indigo ink 5.1:1, the red ink 4.6:1).
- **Reduced transparency is honoured.** Under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` the same theme is drawn on solid ground: no blur, no grain, no wallpaper, on the menu, the page and every control.
- **The system face comes first.** The text stacks start with `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont`, which is SF Pro and SF Arabic on Apple platforms; Inter and IBM Plex Sans Arabic carry every other platform. SF itself is never bundled: its licence covers Apple platforms only.

## Consequences

- The paper theme is unchanged by this record; a light treatment over a pale wallpaper is a later decision.
- Translucent tokens mean a colour's contrast depends on what is behind it, so a new text colour on glass is checked at the wallpaper's brightest point, not against the token alone.
- Blur costs GPU time on old hardware; reduced transparency is the escape hatch, and the panel and dialogs stay legible without it.
